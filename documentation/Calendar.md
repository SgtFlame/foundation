# Calendar

Calendars and scheduling (forge iteration 28, `forge/documentation/plans/028-Calendar.md`). Foundation holds the backend: the time arithmetic, the iCalendar codec, the `calendar.events@1` contract with its test server and suite, and (in later parts) the connector types and the `calendar` library app. Forge holds the Calendar app's pages, Mail's changes, the prefabs and `forge caltest`.

## Built in forge 28a: time zones, recurrence and free time

`features/calendar/when` is the date arithmetic, pure Go over values; `features/calendar/nodes` (`calendarnodes.Contribute`, source `calendar`) puts each piece behind a pure node, contributed from `serve.go` and from Forge's `devtools/catalog`. Nothing here knows about calendars or connectors.

- **The time zone database is embedded** (`time/tzdata`, imported by `when`), so every process agrees whatever its host has installed.
- **A zoned time** is `when.Zoned{local, zone}` (`{"local": "2026-03-29T09:00:00", "zone": "Europe/London"}`), or an all-day date `{"date": "2026-03-29"}` with no zone. `when.Location` accepts IANA ids and Windows zone names (CLDR's mapping, `zones_data.go`); `when.IANA` gives the IANA id.
- `when.Instant(local, loc)` turns a local time into an instant: a local time in the spring gap moves forward by the gap (taking the offset before it), and one in the autumn overlap takes the earlier instant, as RFC 5545 says. `when.LocalOf(t, zone)` gives `{local, zone, offsetMinutes, abbreviation, instant}`.
- **Recurrence**: `when.Recurrence{rrule, rdate, exdate}` expanded by `when.Expand(ExpandArgs{Start, Duration, Recurrence, Window, Max})` over `teambition/rrule-go`, **in the series' own zone**, so a weekly 09:00 London meeting stays 09:00 local across the clock changes. Each `Occurrence` has its start, end, local time and `recurrenceId` (the occurrence's instant as RFC 3339 UTC, or its date for an all-day series). An unbounded rule with no window is refused (`ErrUnbounded`), and more than `Max` occurrences (the `calendar` section's `maxOccurrences`, 1 000) is refused (`ErrTooMany`). `when.SplitRule(start, rule, recurrenceId)` gives the rule ending before an occurrence and the one starting from it (*this and following*), and `when.DescribeRule(rule, zone)` an RRULE as plain English.
- **Free time**: `when.Busy` merges a list of events into busy intervals, ignoring transparent, cancelled and declined ones; `when.FreeSlots(SlotArgs{People, Duration, Window, Step, Before, After, Count, Spread})` gives candidate slots for several people, each with weekly working hours in their own zone (`when.WorkingHours`, `DefaultWorkingHours` Monday to Friday 09:00 to 17:00), skipping each required person's busy time plus buffers, ranked by earliness and by how near the middle of every zone's working day the slot falls (`Spread` takes one a day first); each slot carries its local time for every person.
- **Nodes** (pure): *Convert time zone* (`time.convert`), *List time zones* (`time.zones`), *Expand recurrence* (`time.expand`), *Describe recurrence* (`time.describeRule`), *Split recurrence* (`time.splitRule`), *Busy intervals* (`time.busy`), *Free slots* (`time.freeSlots`). *Expand recurrence* reads its cap from the `calendar` section.
- **Settings**: the `calendar` section (`calendarsettings.Contribute`): `maxOccurrences` (1 000), `workingHours`, the mirror window `pastDays` (90) and `futureDays` (365), `holdDays` (5) and `caldavPollSeconds` (60), read by later parts.

## Built in forge 28b: the iCalendar codec

`features/calendar/ical` reads and writes `.ics` (RFC 5545) and its scheduling methods (iTIP, RFC 5546), over `emersion/go-ical` for reading.

- **Parsing runs only in a child process**, as transcripts do: `ical.Child(exe)` runs the server's own binary with `FOUNDATION_ICAL_CHILD=1` (`ical.ChildRequested()`/`ServeChild()` are called first in Foundation's and Forge's `cli/main.go`), the bytes on stdin and JSON on stdout, limited to 1 MB of input (`ical.MaxBytes`, refused before the child starts), 128 MB of heap and 5 s, two children at once; a crash, a hang or a bomb reports the file unreadable and the server goes on.
- `ical.Parse(raw)` gives `Parsed{method, events, freeBusy, problems}` in the contract's event shape. A `TZID` is resolved as an IANA or Windows name, then by stripping a vendor prefix (`/freeassociation.sourceforge.net/Australia/Sydney`, trying the last three then two path parts), then by the file's `VTIMEZONE` `X-LIC-LOCATION`, then as the fixed offset of its `STANDARD` part. A `RECURRENCE-ID` override becomes an exception of its series (`seriesId`, id `<uid>_<recurrence id>`); the video link comes from `X-GOOGLE-CONFERENCE`, Teams' `X-MICROSOFT-SKYPETEAMSMEETINGURL` or `CONFERENCE`, else from a meeting URL in the text. Fixtures from Google, Outlook, Apple and Fastmail are in `ical/testdata`.
- `ical.Compose(method, events, options)` writes the text in process (our own values): lines folded at 75 octets, text escaped, and a `VTIMEZONE` for each zone used, generated from the zone database's transitions. Methods: `PUBLISH`, `REQUEST`, `REPLY`, `CANCEL`, `COUNTER`, `DECLINECOUNTER`.
- `ical.Reply(invitation, attendee, answer, comment)` is the `REPLY` to send the organizer; `ical.Apply(existing, method, message)` applies an incoming iTIP message, refusing another event (`ErrOtherEvent`), an older `SEQUENCE` (`ErrOlder`), a reply from someone not invited (`ErrNotInvited`), a bad answer (`ErrBadResponse`) or a method it does not apply (`ErrNotApplied`).
- **Nodes**: *Parse iCalendar* (`calendar.parseIcs`, exec, in action, trigger, ui.handler, function, agent and connector.op graphs): a record file reference (read through the files' store after the caller's access check, as *Parse transcript* does) or text gives `method`, `events`, `freeBusy`, `problems`, `ok` and `error`; *Compose iCalendar* (`calendar.composeIcs`), *iTIP reply* (`calendar.itipReply`) and *iTIP apply* (`calendar.itipApply`, giving `applied` and a `reason`: `other`, `older`, `notInvited`, `badResponse`, `notApplied`) are pure. `calendarnodes.ContributeIcal(reg, deps)` wires the parser and the file reader.

## Built in forge 28c: `calendar.events@1`, the Calendar test server and the contract suite

- **The contract** (`calendar.EventsContract()`, `calendar.Contribute`, a Go builtin passing `connectors.CheckContract`): `calendar.list`; `event.list {calendar, window | cursor}` giving `{events, removed, cursor}`, a stale cursor being `invalid` with `cursor_expired` (`calendar.CodeCursorExpired`), after which the caller lists the window again; `event.get`; `event.create` keyed by `key` (a retry makes one event); `event.update {id, etag, set}` (`conflict` when the etag moved); `event.delete` (destructive, idempotent: `deleted` false the second time); `event.respond {id, answer, comment?}` (only the caller's attendee entry); `freeBusy {calendars | people, window}`; `check`. Events `eventChanged`, `eventRemoved` and `calendarsChanged`, each with delivery watch and callback. The event shape is `calendar.Event` (`id`, `etag`, `calendar`, `title`, zoned `start`/`end`, `recurrence`, `recurrenceId` and `seriesId` for an exception, `status`, `transparency`, `location`, `videoLink`, `url`, `organizer`, `attendees` with responses, `description` and `descriptionHtml`, `iCalUID`, `sequence`, `updated`).
- **The Calendar test server** (`calendar/caltest`, type `caltest.calendar`, the `infratest` form): in-memory calendars `primary` and `team` per connection (settings `email`, `name`, `zone`), ids `ct-<n>`, etags counters, the cursor the change count; free/busy over its own events, and for `people` over the calendars of the tenant's other test connections by their email. `caltest.control@1` scripts the outside world: `add`, `move`, `remove` (outside changes, raising their events), `answer` (an invitee answers), `fail {operation, times, kind}`, `expireCursor`, `dropNotifications {n}` (a lost push, caught up through the cursor) and `state`. It runs on the in-process adapter (`connectorImpls()` in `serve.go`, `caltest.Default`). The live call **`caltest.call`** (`caltest.ContributeLive`) runs one operation of either contract on a test connection as the caller, who must be allowed to use it (`Deps.CheckUse`), ending with one `result` event; Forge's `forge caltest` drives it.
- **The contract suite** (`calendar/contracttest.Run(t, call, Options{Calendar, Self, Expire, ReadOnly, NoSeries})`): create is keyed; a stale etag is refused; a window lists what create made and a recurring series once with its rule; the cursor gives only changes and an expired one is `cursor_expired`; delete is idempotent and reported in the delta; respond changes only the caller's entry; free/busy answers. The test server passes it (`caltest_test.go`); 28j, 28k and 28l run it against recorded responses.
- No golden tool-shape files: like the mail contracts, the calendar contract is checked by `CheckContract` and `TestMetadata`.

## Built in forge 28d: `mui.calendar`

A generic primitive for dated items (Tasks and Docs databases reuse it), registered in `features/ui/components/calendar.go` (appended to the primitives in its own file) with its renderer in `frontend/common/src/ui/renderers/mui/calendar/` (its own `contributions.ts`, the chunk loaded lazily).

- **Props**: `items` (`{id, title, start, end, allDay, color, readOnly, group}`, start and end RFC 3339 instants, or dates for all-day items, end exclusive), `view` (`month`, `week` the default, `workWeek`, `day`, `agenda`), `date`, `zone` (the viewer's when left out), `workingHours` (`{start, end}`, shaded and scrolled to), `slotMinutes` (15), `groups` (day-view columns for people or rooms), `selectable`, `editable`, `showNow`, `toolbar`, `height`, `busy` (item id → others on it, drawn as a ring) and `sx`.
- **Events**: `move {id, start, end, group?}` and `resize {id, end}` on drop, `select {start, end, allDay, group?}` over empty slots (in month view, over days), `open {id}`, `range {from, to, view, date}` on first draw and whenever the toolbar changes what is shown (bind the page's query to it), `view {view, date}`.
- **Layout** (`layout.ts`, pure, tested by `yarn test`): wall-clock times in a zone through `Intl` with the server's gap and overlap rules (`instantOf`), each view's days and range, overlapping timed items split their cluster's width (`layoutDay`), all-day and across-midnight items in lanes of a strip above the grid (`layoutStrip`), month cells with *+n more*, the agenda by day over two weeks, and moves snapped to `slotMinutes` keeping an item's wall-clock time across a clock change (`moved`, `movedDays`).
- **Gestures**: a drag shows a ghost and sends one `move` on drop; the item stays where it was dropped until the server's value for it changes (or for 8 seconds, so a refused move goes back); dragging an item's foot sends `resize`; month view moves by whole days with drag and drop. Others' changes are ordinary prop patches. Items are positioned by inline style, never `sx`. Arrow keys move focus between items in time order, Enter opens, and each item's accessible name is its title and time.

## Built in forge 28e: the `calendar` library app

The Calendar app's data and its native calendars, as a library app in `features/calendar/app` (`calendarapp`): `files/app.json`, `files/entities/*.json` and `files/graphs/*.json`, embedded and exposed as `calendarapp.Files` (laid out as a sample app is, which Forge's `starter.LoadApps` seeds into every tenant beside its own library apps) and as an export document (`calendarapp.Document()`, for importing it with `appservice` as `controlplane.SeedApp` does). Forge's Calendar app (28g) requires it.

- **Entity types**: `calCalendar` (name, colour, zone, `owner`, `room`, `connection` (`calendar.events@1`, empty for a native calendar), `outsideId`, `syncCursor`, `windowFrom`/`windowTo` (the mirrored range, dates), `sharedWith` `[{user, level}]`); `calEvent` (28c's event shape as fields: title, `start`/`end` instants (an all-day event's are midnights UTC) and `zone`, `allDay`, `rrule`, `rdate`, `exdate`, `series` and `recurrenceId` for an exception, `status`, `transparency`, location, `videoLink`, organizer, `attendees` `[{email, user, name, response}]`, description, `outsideId`, `etag`, `iCalUID`, `hold`); `calOccurrence` (one row per occurrence in the window: `event`, `series`, `calendar`, `start`, `end`, `allDay`, `recurrenceId`, `status`, `transparency`, written only by the app); `calHold` and `calAvailability` (fields only, for 28h).
- **Access**: a calendar is read by its owner and by whoever holds *Calendar writer*, *Calendar reader* or *Free/busy viewer* on it (per-record app roles `calWriter`, `calReader` and `calFreeBusy`, as Mail's per-account *Member*; named apart from other apps' roles because `principal.hasRole` matches a role by its name in any app); events by writers and readers of their calendar; occurrences also by free/busy viewers, who see no title since the event behind it is hidden (the tools say *Busy*). The trigger `onCalendarShared` grants the maker *Calendar writer* and each `sharedWith` entry its level's role, revoking what was removed; `calendar.calendar.share {user, level freeBusy|read|write|none}` edits `sharedWith`. *Calendar admin* (the installer) sees every calendar but not its events; *Calendar user* is what members get. The per-calendar roles carry row rules over events and occurrences, so an agent granted one on a calendar reaches its events (or only its busy blocks) and nothing else.
- **Series**: a series is its master (with `rrule`, `rdate`, `exdate`) plus exception events keyed by `recurrenceId`. `calendar.event.updateOccurrence {recurrenceId, scope this|following|all, set}`: *this* writes or changes the exception `<master>_x_<slot>`; *following* splits with *Split recurrence* (28a), the master keeping the occurrences before and a new master `<master>_f_<slot>` taking the rest with the later exceptions; *all* changes the master. `calendar.event.respond {answer, comment}` sets the caller's own attendee entry.
- **Occurrences**: `calendar.occurrences.sync` (sent by the app) expands a master in its zone over the calendar's window (`windowFrom`/`windowTo`, else 90 days back and 365 ahead), puts exceptions in their slots, and creates, updates and deletes occurrence rows with fixed ids (`<master>_<slot>`), checking each row by id first, so a second sync changes nothing even when the occurrence query has not caught up. A command graph runs at most 200 nodes, so the sync works on a slice given as `from`/`to` (slots in that range): the job `calendar-syncEvent` plans the 14-day slices that need one (every slice for a series; for a one-off, those holding its start or an occurrence it had) and sends a sync per slice. `onEventChanged` enqueues that job for the event's series, keyed by it (one series' syncs run one at a time), and `onWindow` enqueues it daily for every master, its job id naming the day.
- **Tools** (the manifest's `tools`, for chat and agents): `calendar.listEvents {from, to, calendars?}` (occurrences in time order with title, location and times, *Busy* where only free/busy is shared), `calendar.createEvent` and `calendar.updateEvent` (`recurrenceId` and `scope` for a series), all as the caller.
- Tested by Forge's `go test ./features/starter/calendar` (series with moved and cancelled occurrences, *this and following*, a New York series over both clock changes, two writers racing, free/busy to a person and an agent, the window sliding with the job run twice, a daily series over thirteen months) and `go test ./features/calendar/app`.

## Built in forge 28f: two-way sync with outside calendars

A connected calendar mirrors its outside events into `calEvent` records within its window and sends edits made in Axiom back, whatever the connector type, all as graphs of the `calendar` library app. Built and tested against the Calendar test server; the real types (28j, 28k, 28l) only implement `calendar.events@1`.

- **Connecting**: the tool `calendar.connect {connection, calendars?}` (as the caller, who must be allowed to use the connection) lists the connection's calendars and makes a `calCalendar` for each one picked (every one when none are named), owned by the caller, with `connection` and `outsideId`, skipping those already connected. The trigger `onConnected` (a calendar made with a connection, or given one) enqueues one full sync. The app's own jobs then use the connection because a record of its own type holds it (`connections.Held`).
- **Inbound**: the job `calendar-pull {calendar, full}` (keyed by the calendar, so a burst of notices runs one sync at a time) calls `event.list` from the stored `syncCursor`, or over the window when `full` is set or no cursor is held, and sends each listed event, and each removed id it holds, to `calendar.event.mirror` (a command, sent as the app). A full sync also removes the mirrored events the outside no longer lists (series, and one-offs starting in the window). Then it stores the new cursor, `lastSync` and `syncState: ok`. An expired cursor (`cursor_expired`) enqueues one full sync (its job id names the expired cursor, so two processes enqueue it once); a retryable failure sets `syncState: behind` and the job is retried; any other sets `error` with `syncError`.
- **Which record an outside event goes to**: the record of that calendar already holding its `outsideId`; else, for an event made here (its `iCalUID` is `<record id>@axiom`), that record when it is the same calendar's; else `<calendar>_o_<base64url of the outside id>`, so mirroring an event twice finds one record. An exception's `series` is found the same way from its `seriesId`. `calendar.event.mirror {calendar, event, removed?, series?}` makes the record when new, changes only the fields that differ when the etag moved, does nothing when it holds that etag already, deletes on `removed`, and refuses a target that is another calendar's.
- **Notices**: the triggers `onOutsideChanged` and `onOutsideRemoved` (*On connector event* for `eventChanged` and `eventRemoved`) enqueue a delta pull of each calendar mirroring the noticed one; `onCatchUp` enqueues a delta of every connected calendar daily (job id `catchup_<day>_<calendar>`), so a lost notice is caught up within a day, or within the poll of a type whose `Watch` polls. The payload is never trusted: every change is read through `event.list`.
- **Outbound**: `onEventEdited` (*On record changed* on `calEvent`, without `ownChanges`) enqueues the job `calendar-push` for each change a person or an agent makes on a connected calendar, keyed by the event, its job id naming the change. The app's own writes (mirrored changes, ids written back) start nothing, so an edit never bounces back; `onEventChanged` takes `ownChanges`, so mirrored events get their occurrences. The push sends `event.create` keyed by the record's id with `iCalUID` `<id>@axiom` (a retried job makes one outside event) and writes back `outsideId` and `etag` as the app; `event.update` with the whole event and the etag the edit was based on, writing back the new etag and clearing `refusedChange`; `event.delete` for a deleted event. A stale etag gets `conflict`: the outside version is kept (a delta pull is enqueued) and the edit is recorded on the event as `refusedChange {set, at, reason}`, for the event editor to show.
- **Status fields**: `calCalendar.lastSync`, `syncState` (`ok`, `behind`, `error`) and `syncError`; a connection in `error` makes the dispatcher refuse the jobs' calls until it is fixed.
- **Not yet**: `calendarsChanged` starts nothing (calendars are listed again by connecting); an edit made while an earlier push of the same event has not written back its outside id may be pushed with the record as it was then.
- Tested by Forge's `go test ./features/starter/calendar` (`sync_test.go`, the fixture serving the test server in an adapter): connecting mirrors the window once and a second full and delta sync rewrite nothing; an outside move and removal arrive through `Watch`; an event made, moved and deleted here reaches the test server once even when the update is retried, and comes back as the same record; a dropped notice is caught up by the next delta; an expired cursor causes one full sync without duplicates; a stale etag keeps the outside version and records the refused change; four delta jobs on two runners write a change once.

## Built in forge 28h: free time, holds and the scheduling tools

Finding times that suit several people and holding them while a proposal is open, as tools of the `calendar` library app (function graphs over 28a's *Free slots*), for agents and the chat; a proposal never sends anything itself (28i and 28m carry it).

- **Free time** (`calendar.findFreeTime {people, durationMinutes, from, to, count?}`, `fn-calendar-findFreeTime`): the calling person and the people named (user ids). A person's busy time is the opaque, not cancelled occurrences of the calendars they **own** (`calCalendar.owner`; team calendars count for nobody) that the caller may see, so a free/busy share is enough and gives no titles; their hours are their own `calAvailability` (`zone`, `workingHours` as `{mon: [{start, end}], …}`, `bufferMinutes`), else the `calendar` section's `workingHours`; the largest buffer applies before and after. Slots are spread one a day first, each with its time in every person's zone; people with no calendar the caller can see come back in `unknown` and count as free.
- **Holds** (`calHold {title, calendar, slots: [{start, end, event}], status open|chosen|released, expires, subject}`): `calendar.hold {slots, purpose, subject?, calendar?, zone?}` (`fn-calendar-hold`) writes the Hold and a `tentative` event per slot on the caller's first own calendar (or the one named), with ids `<hold>_s<n>` and `hold` set, so a connected calendar sends them outside through 28f's push; it expires after the section's `holdDays`. `calendar.choose {hold, slot}` confirms the slot's event (clearing `hold`), deletes the others, then marks the Hold chosen; `calendar.release {hold}` deletes them all and marks it released. Both leave a settled Hold alone and finish an interrupted run when retried. The hourly trigger `onHoldsExpire` releases open Holds past `expires`, as the app.
- **Proposals** (`calendar.proposeMeeting {people, durationMinutes, from, to, purpose, subject?, count?}`): finds slots and holds them, returning each slot with its held `event`, the `hold` and `expires`; an agent whose `agentRecord.autonomy` is `suggest` gets the slots only.
- **Answers** (`calendar.respond {event, answer, comment?}`): the caller's own answer through `calendar.event.respond`.
- Tested end to end in Forge's `features/starter/calendar` (`freetime_test.go`): three people in three zones, a hold seen busy by a colleague's free/busy, choosing twice, releasing, expiry by the trigger, a suggesting agent, and a hold on a Calendar test server calendar reaching it as tentative events.

## Changed in forge 28i: proposals for an agent's maker, and open holds

Part 28i's first step (Forge's Mail app proposes times by mail; Forge `MailApp.md` › *Built in part 28i*).

- **An agent proposes for the person who made it**: `calendar.findFreeTime` and `calendar.proposeMeeting` called by an agent that names nobody take `agentRecord._createdBy` as the people. `proposeMeeting` holds on the given `calendar`, else for an agent on the first calendar that person owns (not a room) that the agent can see, in that calendar's zone unless `zone` is given. The person gives the agent *Calendar writer* (`calWriter`) on that calendar. That role now also holds `entity:calHold.*`, with the row rule `principal.hasRole('calWriter', record.calendar)`.
- **`calendar.hold`** for a caller who is not a user (an agent) no longer queries calendars owned by `""`. `proposeMeeting`'s slots-only answer carries `error` when the hold failed.
- **Open holds** (`calendar.holds {subject}`, `fn-calendar-holds`, read): the open Holds of a proposal's subject (`{entity, id}`, a mail thread) that the caller may see, each with its slots and their event ids. An agent reads them to tell which time a reply picks before `calendar.choose`.
